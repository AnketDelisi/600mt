Methodology

How the model
works.

A race-by-race forecast built from presidential lean, district fundamentals, polls and a correlated national/region/state simulation — backtested on 2018–24 and run again every day.

The forecast in one paragraph

Senate races start from the state's presidential lean, adjusted for the national environment (scaled by the state's historical elasticity), incumbency and trend. House races start from the district's presidential margin on the 2026 lines, the last House result and the rating band, with the historical generic-ballot-to-House-vote gap corrected. Governors use a 50/50 blend of the current and prior state presidential lean, the rating band and incumbency. Polls are rebuilt from individual surveys with recency, partisan, sample-size, population, pollster-rating, one-poll-per-pollster and house-effect weights, then blended against the fundamentals with an outlier guard.

How to read the numbers

Margin is the model's two-party D−R margin; positive is Democratic. Win probability is the share of 20,000 simulated election nights the party wins the race or chamber. Seat distribution counts Democratic seats in those nights, in two-seat buckets; the marker is the majority line. Polling average is the rebuilt poll number, not a raw average. Races without public polling use fundamentals alone and show a dash.

Backtest

The 2024 cycle is held out from parameter selection; 2018–22 are the fit cycles. Margin MAE is in points; seat error is expected minus actual Democratic seats.

Data & cadence

Races, ratings and generic-ballot polls: Wikipedia 2026 articles; the generic environment is a quality-weighted average of the aggregator rows. District presidential results: The Downballot. Historical state swings: county presidential results 2008–24. Pollster ratings: Silver Bulletin. Candidate fundraising: FEC bulk data. Boundaries: Census TIGERweb, clipped to Natural Earth land and lakes. The pipeline runs daily at 07:00 UTC; the model timestamp lives in data/us/forecast.json.
